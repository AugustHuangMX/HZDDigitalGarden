First, we find the partial derivative of the labor supply function with respect to the wage, w:

$$\frac{\partial h}{\partial w} = \frac{\partial}{\partial w} \left( \frac{\alpha T}{\alpha+1} - \frac{y}{w(\alpha+1)} \right) = \frac{y}{w^2(\alpha+1)}$$ Now, we can find the elasticity: $$\epsilon = \left(\frac{y}{w^2(\alpha+1)}\right) \frac{w}{h} = \frac{y}{w(\alpha+1)} \frac{1}{\frac{\alpha wT - y}{w(\alpha+1)}} = \frac{y}{\alpha wT - y}$$ To find the **compensated wage elasticity**, $\epsilon^c$, we use the Slutsky equation in elasticity form from the slides[cite: 135]: $$\epsilon = \epsilon^c + w \frac{\partial h}{\partial y}$$ We first need the partial derivative of labor supply with respect to unearned income, $y$: $$\frac{\partial h}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( \frac{\alpha wT - y}{w(\alpha+1)} \right) = -\frac{1}{w(\alpha+1)}$$ Now, we rearrange the Slutsky equation to solve for $\epsilon^c$:

$$\epsilon^c = \epsilon - w \frac{\partial h}{\partial y} = \frac{y}{\alpha wT - y} - w \left( -\frac{1}{w(\alpha+1)} \right)$$



$$\epsilon^c = \frac{y}{\alpha wT - y} + \frac{1}{\alpha+1}$$ To combine these two parts, we find a common denominator, which is $(\alpha wT - y)(\alpha+1)$: $$\epsilon^c = \frac{y(\alpha+1) + 1(\alpha wT - y)}{(\alpha wT - y)(\alpha+1)}$$ Now, we expand the numerator: $$\epsilon^c = \frac{\alpha y + y + \alpha wT - y}{(\alpha wT - y)(\alpha+1)}$$ The $+y$ and $-y$ terms in the numerator cancel, leaving: $$\epsilon^c = \frac{\alpha y + \alpha wT}{(\alpha wT - y)(\alpha+1)}$$ Finally, we can factor out $\alpha$ from the numerator to get the final expression for the **compensated wage elasticity**: $$\epsilon^c = \frac{\alpha(wT+y)}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha wT - y)}$$



$$\epsilon' \bigg|_{h' \to 0} = \lim_{h' \to 0} \left( \frac{\partial h'}{\partial w'} \frac{w'}{h'} \right)$$Since $\frac{\partial h'}{\partial w'}$ at $w'=w$ is a positive constant and $w>0$, the limit is:$$\epsilon' \bigg|_{h' \to 0} = \left(\frac{y+wh^*}{w^2(\alpha+1)}\right) \cdot \lim_{h' \to 0} \left(\frac{w}{h'}\right) = \infty$$
