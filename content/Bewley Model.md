#LSE/EC417_Macroeconomics 

The key difference between [[Bewley Model]], [[Aiyagari Model]], and [[Huggett Model]] lies in the presence of Government bonds.

In [[Bewley Model]], government isn't invisible. It has following actions:

- $G$: Government buys [[Public Goods]].
- $B$: Government issues bonds to finance its spending.  And *Households* buy these bonds as a way of saving.
- $T$: Taxes collected from households to pay for government spending and interest on bonds.

The  total tax revenues are:

$$
T(r) = \sum_{j} \int_{a} \tau(a,y_{j}) g(a,y_{j} ;r) da
$$

It's intuitive that the current statues $a$ and $y_{j}$ would lead to different tax payments $\tau(\cdot)$.

Thus government faces its own budget constraint:

$$
G + rB = T(r)
$$

where $rB$ would be the net borrowing cost on government bonds.

The market clearing condition thus is simply:

$$
A(r) = B
$$

meaning that all saving by households would be absurd by government bonds.
