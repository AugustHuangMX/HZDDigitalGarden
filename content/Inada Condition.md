#Econ/Macro 


The intuition behind the [[Inada Condition]] mainly concerns about *marginal product of capital*, ie, $MPK$. It is the first order derivative of the production function with respect to capital, mathematically, we denoted by $F_{k}(k,h)$ or $f'(k)$.

>  $h$ here means the labor input, and $k$ means the capital input.

The two key [[Inada Condition]] are:

1. $\lim_{ k \to 0} F_{k}(k,h) = \infty$: As the amount of capital of input is closed to $0$, the marginal product of capital would be extremely high, ie, $MPK \to \infty$. It means that if a country has very little capital, then investing in capital would bring a very high return.
2. $\lim_{ k \to \infty} F_{k}(k,h) = 0$: This one is also very intuitive, as the amount of capital of input is extremely high, the marginal product of capital would be very low, ie, $MPK \to 0$. It means that if a country has a lot of capital, then investing in capital would bring a very low return.
