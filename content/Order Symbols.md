#Econ/metrics 

There are two kinds of [[Order Symbols]], one is the "small oh", and another is the "big Oh".

# The small oh

Let $\{c_{n}\}$ and $\{a_{n}\}$ be sequence of real numbers, then 

- $c_{n} \to o(1)$ is same as $c_{n} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.
- $c_{n} = o(a_{n})$ means that $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{c_{n}}{a_{n}} = 0$. 

>  Intuitively, $c_{n}$ goes to zero faster than $a_{n}$.


The motivation of this notation is that, suppose $w_{n} = o(n)$, it means that $w$ grows no faster than $n$, for example.
